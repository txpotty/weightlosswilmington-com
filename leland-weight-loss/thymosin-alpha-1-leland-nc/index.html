    How Thymosin Alpha-1 Works in Leland

    Thymosin Alpha-1 is a chain of 28 amino acids first pulled from thymus tissue in the 1970s. In lab and clinical studies it appears to help T cells mature. It also seems to adjust cytokine signals, the chemical messages immune cells use to coordinate a response to viruses and other threats. Think of it as a signal, not a shield. The synthetic form, thymalfasin, is approved abroad for hepatitis B, but the FDA has not approved it in the US.

    Who Usually Asks About It

    Typical candidates are adults who have read about immune peptides and want a clinician to weigh in before trying one. A few describe a season of back-to-back infections and slow recovery. Others already use BPC-157 or NAD+ and want a provider to look at the whole stack, including supplements and prescription drugs they take every day. It does not replace vaccines. An active infection still needs standard treatment from your regular clinician, and your provider will say so if your intake suggests one.

    Dosing and Schedule in Leland

    Side Effects and Safety in Leland

    In published trials, the most frequent side effect was mild redness or discomfort at the injection site. A small number describe feeling achy or tired for about a day after a dose.
