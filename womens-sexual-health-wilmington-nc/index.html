    Understanding Low Desire in Wilmington

    Low sexual desire is one of the most common sexual health concerns women report. Persistent, distressing low desire that is not better explained by something else is called hypoactive sexual desire disorder, or HSDD. Causes usually overlap. SSRIs, birth control, perimenopause, thyroid issues, pain with sex, sleep and relationship stress all play roles. A good evaluation looks at each.

    Low Libido Treatment for Women

    Treatment starts with finding what changed. Your provider reviews medications known to lower desire, asks about menopause symptoms, thyroid history and pain with sex, and looks at sleep and stress. Sometimes the answer is small. Treating vaginal dryness or adjusting an SSRI with your prescriber can make a real difference before any new medication is added. Medication options follow if needed.

    Flibanserin for Women in Wilmington

    Flibanserin, sold as Addyi, is FDA-approved for premenopausal women with acquired, generalized HSDD. It is a non-hormonal 100 mg tablet taken once daily at bedtime that acts on serotonin and dopamine in the brain. Results build slowly. If nothing has improved by about eight weeks, it is typically stopped. Alcohol timing matters with flibanserin.

    PT-141 for Women

    PT-141, or bremelanotide, is FDA-approved as Vyleesi for premenopausal women with acquired, generalized HSDD. You inject 1.75 mg under the skin of the abdomen or thigh at least 45 minutes ahead of sex, and it works on brain receptors tied to desire. Nausea is common at first. Use it no more than once in 24 hours or eight times a month. Compounded versions are not FDA-approved.

    Oxytocin Therapy in Wilmington

    Oxytocin is a hormone involved in bonding, touch and arousal. Compounded oxytocin is sometimes prescribed as a nasal spray or troche taken before intimacy to support arousal and closeness. This use is off-label. There is no FDA approval for this use, the evidence is thin and responses differ a lot from woman to woman. Your provider explains the evidence honestly.

    How a Provider Chooses Between Options

    Menopausal status comes first. Flibanserin and Vyleesi are approved for premenopausal women, so postmenopausal women may be offered off-label options, hormonal approaches or a referral instead. Then comes routine. Women who drink regularly may do better with PT-141, while women with high blood pressure may do better with flibanserin, and liver health matters for flibanserin. Your preferences count as well.

    Side Effects and Safety in Wilmington

    Flibanserin can cause dizziness, sleepiness, nausea and low blood pressure. Those effects are stronger with alcohol or with drugs that block CYP3A4, which is why bedtime dosing and alcohol limits are part of the label. PT-141 causes nausea often. It also causes flushing and headache, briefly raises blood pressure and can darken skin with repeated use. Oxytocin can cause headache or nasal irritation.
